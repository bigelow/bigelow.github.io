# VOICE.md — standing rules for anything published under my name

Three rules. Every draft — mine, Claude's, anyone's — gets checked
against them before it ships.

## 1. Claims sized to evidence, always

"Verified against v1.36" only when there is a run to point at.
"Documented but not implemented" said plainly when that's the truth.
If a sentence claims more than the files or the logs support, shrink
the sentence — never inflate the evidence. Scope statements ("this
covers X; Y is out of scope") are a feature, not a hedge.

## 2. No verdict theater

Cut: "the verdict is straightforward," "winning organizations,"
"game-changer," bold-pronouncement closers, emoji signposts, and any
sentence whose job is to sound authoritative rather than to say
something true. The natural ending is the quiet kind — a true
observation, not a rallying cry. Don't caption your own work
impressive; let the reader infer.

## 3. First person, working engineer

"I built," "I missed this," "trivy caught what I walked past."
Own specific errors by name; they are the trust signature. Pair
self-effacement about mistakes with zero hedging on verified claims.
Never passive-voice a miss ("it had only ever been configured") when
the honest sentence is "I configured it but never ran it."

When in doubt, read a paragraph of it aloud and match the register:
plain declarative sentences, dry rather than clever, claims exactly
the size of their evidence.
